Name of the Unit/Lesson: CH. 1 Place Value Subject: Mathematics Grade: IV

Aim of the Lesson: The main aim of teaching place value is to ensure students comprehend the value of digits based on their positions in large numbers and can apply this understanding to read, write, compare, order, and round numbers confidently.

Objectives: The main aim of teaching place value is to ensure learners develop a strong understanding of how each digit’s position affects the overall value of a number and can confidently read and write large numbers using both the Indian and International systems of numeration. Throughout this lesson, students will explore representations of five‑ and six‑digit numbers in figures and words, construct numbers in expanded and standard forms, and apply their knowledge to compare, order, and round numbers accurately. To enrich their understanding and number sense, students will also engage with Roman numerals, recognizing their formation rules and converting between Roman and Hindu‑Arabic numerals.

Outcomes: By the end of the lesson, students will be able to recognize and articulate the value of any digit based on its positional context within a number and create, read, and write numbers up to 1,00,000 in both numbering systems. They will differentiate between face value and place value, perform accurate comparison and ordering of large numbers, and round off numbers to the nearest ten, hundred, and thousand with confidence. Additionally, students will interpret and write Roman numerals up to 100 and apply these skills in practical problem‑solving scenarios.

Resources Used: Chart paper; number cards (0–9); place value cards (ones to lakhs); Oxford textbook; video link: [https://youtu.be/6d43vYG0Evo](https://youtu.be/6d43vYG0Evo); Quizizz link: [https://quizizz.com/admin/quiz/57c9763b61c58cbfae1606f2/place-value-3rd-grade](https://quizizz.com/admin/quiz/57c9763b61c58cbfae1606f2/place-value-3rd-grade)

Formative Assessments: Written exercises; Quizizz quiz; worksheets; slip test; chapter checkup.

TUNE

TUNE

Objective: To activate prior knowledge and pique student interest in place value concepts.

Activities: • Recall exercise: Complete the sentences: 10 ones = ____ tens; 10 tens = ____ hundreds; 10 hundreds = ____ thousands. • Oral practice: Write the following numbers in words: 5698; 7876. • Quick comparison: Fill in <, >, or =: 25 ____ XV; XIX ____ 38; 18 ____ XVIII. • Video viewing: Watch the form-and-read-numbers video and discuss key points.

RESEARCH

RESEARCH

Objective: To explore number construction and reinforce understanding of place value through hands-on activity.

Activities: • Group activity with number cards (0–9) and place value cards (ones to lakhs). – Each group draws five number cards and forms the greatest 5-digit number. – Use place value cards to identify face value and place value of each digit. – Form the smallest 5-digit number with the same cards and write its number name. – Points awarded for accuracy; highest-scoring group wins.

INVESTIGATE

INVESTIGATE

Objective: To deepen understanding of place value terminology and number systems.

Activities: • Teacher-led explanation with chart: – Define place value and face value. – Present place value chart in Indian and International systems; explain periods and comma separators. • Concept mapping: – Ascending vs. descending order; examples on board. • Rounding rules demonstration: – Round given numbers to nearest 10, 100, 1,000. – Practice problems: 3257 → nearest ten, hundred, thousand. • Introduction to Roman numerals 1–100: – Explain formation rules; practice conversion exercises.

UNDERSTAND

UNDERSTAND

Objective: To apply place value concepts in real-life contexts and integrate across subjects.

Activities: • Social studies integration: – Compare populations of two states; calculate difference; form and compare numbers. • Science integration: – Compare diameters of Earth and Mercury; label on a scale diagram. • Life skills: – Estimate attendance for a class party by rounding off expected numbers. • Global perspective: – Write expanded form of forest area (in km²) of five Asian countries. • Higher-order thinking: – Discuss why rounding is useful in daily life; give examples.

MEASURE

MEASURE

Objective: To assess student learning and comprehension of place value concepts.

Activities: • Worksheets covering: – Expanded form and standard form. – Ascending and descending order. – Rounding exercises. – Roman numeral conversions. • Quizizz online assessment. • Question bank: – MCQs, case-based questions, long and short answers, H.O.T. tasks.

PROGRESS

PROGRESS

Objective: To encourage higher-order thinking skills and student reflection.

Activities: • Problem-solving scenario: – Given digit sums, form numbers and determine the greatest; write number names. • Oral recapitulation: – Key questions: What is place value? How do you round to the nearest thousand? • Reflective discussion on strategies and challenges.

HARMONIZE

HARMONIZE

Objective: To consolidate learning and foster creative connections.

Activities: • Reflection journal: – Students write what they learned about place value and its importance. • Creative poster: – Illustrate the place value chart with examples and visuals. • Real-world project: – In groups, find and document real-world examples of place value (e.g., prices, measurements) and present to class.
